Tutorial #2 — Basic Brain Decoding on EEG Data

Decoding four imagined movements from the BCI Competition IV 2a benchmark — the reference dataset for motor-imagery BCIs. This first step is all about understanding the data.

About the dataset · BCI Competition IV 2a

What it is: real EEG from 9 people who repeatedly imagined moving one of four body parts. It was recorded to a high standard by the Graz BCI Lab for the 2008 BCI Competition IV, and it is the reference benchmark for motor-imagery decoding — when a paper says "tested on BCI IV 2a," this is it. In MOABB/braindecode it loads under the id BNCI2014_001.

The task — four imagined movements

Each trial, the person imagines one movement without actually moving. Four classes means chance = 25%.

LabelImagined movement
0Left hand
1Right hand
2Both feet
3Tongue

Who and how much

  • 9 subjects (A01–A09), all healthy.
  • 2 sessions per subject, recorded on different days — one for training (0train), one for testing (1test). Training on one day and testing on another is what makes the benchmark honest and hard: the model must generalize across sessions.
  • 288 trials per session, 72 per class, in random order.

The recording setup

  • 22 EEG electrodes over sensorimotor cortex (10–20 layout), plus 3 EOG (eye) channels that are dropped before decoding.
  • 250 Hz sampling → 4 ms per sample.
  • Hardware band-pass 0.5–100 Hz and a 50 Hz notch at recording time.

The trial timing (Graz paradigm)

Each ~7.5 s trial: a fixation cross and beep at t = 0; at t = 2 s an arrow cue shows which movement to imagine; the person sustains the imagery until t = 6 s; then a short rest. The cue is the label.

From raw signal to a trainable dataset

This example reproduces the braindecode pipeline. For each subject it:

  • keeps the 22 EEG channels and converts volts → µV,
  • band-passes 4–38 Hz (the motor-imagery band),
  • applies exponential moving standardization so each channel is normalized on the fly,
  • cuts trial windows starting 0.5 s before the cue → each window is 1125 samples = 4.5 s, shape [22 channels × 1125 samples], one of 4 labels.

A full run of the reference model (ShallowFBCSPNet) reaches ~68% accuracy on the held-out session for a single subject — vs 25% chance. That known target is part of why this dataset is so useful: you can tell whether your run is working.

Heads-up for the Jetson: unlike Tutorial #1's tiny sample, the first time you pick a subject the data downloads (hundreds of MB), and 22×1125 windows are much heavier to train — this is where the GPU earns its keep. The dataset needs moabb installed in the container (pip install moabb).
